About these numbers
  • Source. CDC National Center for Health Statistics, VSRR Provisional County-Level Drug Overdose Death Counts (data.cdc.gov dataset gb4e-yj24), downloaded with data as of October 4, 2026. Statewide line: VSRR Provisional Drug Overdose Death Counts (xkb8-kh2a), reported values.
  • What counts. Deaths with an underlying cause of drug poisoning (ICD-10 X40–X44, X60–X64, X85, Y10–Y14): accidental, intentional, assault and undetermined intent, all drugs. Not opioid-specific.
  • Rolling 12 months. Each value is the total for the 12 months ending in that month, which smooths seasonality but lags turning points by several months.
  • Provisional. Recent months can rise as pending death investigations close, especially for counties with many pending toxicology results.
  • County vs. statewide. County counts are by county of residence; the CDC statewide series is by place of occurrence, so the two are close but not identical.
  • Population. U.S. Census Bureau Vintage 2024 county estimates (July 1 of each year). Months in 2025–2026 use the 2024 estimate, so recent rates are slightly overstated in fast-growing counties.
  • Suppression. CDC withholds counts between 1 and 9; those appear blank here and are left out of totals.
  • Neighborhood map. CDC Mapping Injury, Overdose, and Violence Dashboard, census tract data (data.cdc.gov 4day-mt2f), unintentional and undetermined-intent drug overdose deaths by tract of residence, grouped by CDC to at least 10,000 residents. Boundaries: 2020 Census tracts (Census cartographic files), dissolved into CDC’s groups. The map counts a slightly narrower set of deaths than the county figures (it excludes suicides and assaults). One downtown Miami group (tracts 26, 28 and 31) shows 50 and 157 deaths in 2022 and 2023, far above any other area; it may reflect how residence was recorded and is worth verifying before drawing conclusions.
  • Treatment programs. SAMHSA FindTreatment.gov locator, federally certified opioid treatment programs (OTPs) in the three counties, downloaded November 13, 2026. "Methadone clinics" are programs listing outpatient methadone maintenance (10 sites); the other OTPs offer detox, residential or inpatient care and may use methadone only for short-term withdrawal. SAMHSA's OTP Directory lists about 40 certified programs in these counties; roughly 18 of them are not in FindTreatment.gov with a location and are not shown. Medicaid acceptance is as reported to FindTreatment.gov. Distance to the nearest methadone clinic is a straight line from each area's geographic center, not travel time by car or transit.
  • Free naloxone sites. Fixed places only, compiled November 17, 2026: county health department clinics (DOH-Miami-Dade 2025 and DOH-Broward and DOH-Palm Beach 2024 public lists), two distributors named by DOH-Palm Beach and the ISAVEFL database (The Palm Beach Hub, BARC), the Broward Sheriff's Office jail-lobby vending machines (installed 2025), and Hanley Foundation emergency Narcan cabinets (their public sheet). Not shown: the C. L. Brumback Health Center in Belle Glade, three Hanley cabinets (Club Oasis, HomeSafe, Genesis Community Health) and BSO's Lauderdale Lakes office machine, whose addresses could not be placed reliably; Lauderhill Fire Rescue's 30+ city dispensers (no public list); pharmacies (all can dispense under the statewide standing order); mail-based programs; and the wider ISAVEFL network of enrolled distributors. Some cabinets are in recovery residences or supportive housing and may be open to residents only. Sites change often; call ahead.
  • Where deaths occurred. Public sub-county data place deaths by residence only. Place of death (home, hospital, other) is available for counties through CDC WONDER and for some districts in medical examiner reports, not by neighborhood.
  • Not shown yet. Non-fatal overdoses (ED visits, EMS), drug-specific deaths and medical-examiner toxicology are not in these files; they come from FDOH FLHealthCHARTS, DOH-Palm Beach OD2A and the FDLE Medical Examiners Commission.
  • Data files. Every number on this page comes from the CSV and JSON files in the data folder of this site.
Built from public data: CDC National Center for Health Statistics, U.S. Census Bureau, SAMHSA, Florida Department of Health county offices, Broward Sheriff's Office and the Hanley Foundation. In an emergency call 911. For treatment referral, call the SAMHSA National Helpline, 1-800-662-4357.
